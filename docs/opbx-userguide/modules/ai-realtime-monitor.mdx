# AI Real Time Monitor

The AI Real Time Monitor shows, at a glance, how your [AI assistants](/opbx/modules/ai-assistants) and [AI load balancers](/opbx/modules/ai-load-balancers) are doing right now. It runs as a slide-show, so it can stay open on a wall screen: the **Assistants** view and the **Load Balancers** view take turns every 30 seconds.

Open it from **AI Configuration → Real Time Monitor**. Only the **Owner** and **PBX Admin** roles see it.

## Controls

| Control | What it does |
|---------|--------------|
| Time frame | The period the figures cover: the last 1, 5, 15 or 30 minutes, or the last 1, 3, 6 or 12 hours. The default is the last 60 minutes. Your choice is remembered in this browser. |
| Assistants / Load Balancers | Jump to a view. The 30-second countdown starts again. |
| ◀ ▶ | Previous or next view. |
| Pause / Resume | Stop the slide-show on the current view, or start it again. |

The figures refresh every 10 seconds. While the browser tab is hidden, the slide-show and the refresh both wait. The bar at the bottom counts down to the next view.

Cards are laid out up to three across. With more assistants or load balancers than fit on the screen, the page scrolls; the slide-show keeps alternating between the two views.

## The Assistants view

One card per AI assistant. The coloured edge on the left shows its health over the time frame:

| Edge | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Green | Fewer than 5% of its finished calls failed |
| Amber | 5% to 20% failed |
| Red | 20% or more failed. The card is also tinted. |
| Grey | No calls in the time frame |

| Figure | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Success ring | Answered calls as a share of the calls that finished |
| Active now | Calls the assistant has right now |
| Calls | Calls handed to the assistant in the time frame |
| Avg duration | Average talk time of its answered calls (minutes:seconds) |
| AI minutes | Total talk time of its answered calls |
| Last call | When it last got a call, even if that is before the time frame. A long gap points to an assistant nobody is reaching. |
| Chart | Calls over the time frame, oldest on the left |

## The Load Balancers view

One card per AI load balancer, with the same colours. Its health is the share of calls that ended in the load balancer's **fallback**.

| Figure | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Handled ring | Calls that an AI assistant kept: they did not end in the fallback |
| Active now | Calls in the load balancer right now |
| Calls | Calls that reached the load balancer in the time frame |
| Failovers | Times a member failed and **Follow Through** passed the call to the next member |
| Fell back | Calls where no member could take the call and the fallback ran |
| Member bars | Each member's share of the load balancer's calls. For the **percentage** strategy the marker shows the share you configured (from the weights), for **round robin** an equal share. For **priority** the member's priority is shown instead. |

Comparing a member's bar with its marker shows whether the load balancer is spreading calls the way it is set up to.

## What counts as a call

A call counts once for every AI assistant it is handed to. When a load balancer moves a call from a failed member to the next one, the failed member gets a failed call and the next member gets a new one.

A call counts as **answered** when its call record (CDR) says it connected. AI minutes count only the time after the call reached that assistant.

**Auto-dialer campaigns** count only calls where a person answered: voicemail detection said *human*, or the campaign does not use voicemail detection. Calls that reached voicemail, or where detection could not decide, are left out.

The monitor starts counting from the day it was installed: earlier calls do not appear. Its data is kept for 30 days (`RETENTION_AI_CALL_ATTEMPTS_DAYS` in `.env`).

## API

`GET /v1/ai-monitor?minutes=60` returns the same figures (see the [REST API](/opbxRestOpenAPI)). API keys need the **AI Monitor** (`ai-monitor`) grant; the AI Assistants and AI Load Balancers grants do not cover it, because the monitor shows both.
